# Voxels as modelling primitives: V.4 (rigid body from the grid) and V.5 (destruction). Design note, no code.

Netuno, 2026-10-07. For Urano. Facts about the code are read from it on main 8508fa6 (+ feat/granular-bed); things I did not measure are marked NOT MEASURED. Nothing here is built.

## 0. What exists and what changes the design

- **Rapier 0.36 / parry 0.31 have a native voxel shape.** `ColliderBuilder::voxels(voxel_size, &[IVector])`, `parry::shape::Voxels` (sparse chunks, `set_voxel(key, filled)`, per-axis `voxel_size`, `scaled`), contact manifolds voxels-voxels / voxels-ball / voxels-convex / voxels-composite (the trimesh ground is a composite), neighbour state for internal faces (`voxels_neighborhood.rs`), and `mass_properties_voxels(density, voxels)` = sum of cuboids by the parallel-axis theorem. This is the collider; the greedy-box compound and the hull are fallbacks.
- **Today a rigid body is a `Body3Spec { shape, mass, .. }` and the collider gets `.mass(b.mass)`** (physics3d.rs:666): the inertia is the shape's scaled to that mass. For a voxel body that is wrong unless the shape's own inertia is the union of cubes' (it is, with `.density()`, see 1) or `mass_properties` is given.
- **The rigid world is built once from a fixed list of bodies** (`World3::new`, `st.handles`, `active` flags) and reached at any time by replay from checkpoints (a `State` clone each `steps_per_checkpoint`, budgeted by `checkpoint_charge`). Nothing spawns a body at run time. The fracture pieces avoid it: `fracture::Geometry.pieces` are bodies that exist from the start and are enabled (`frame.fractured`) when the parent breaks. This is the constraint that shapes 2.
- The ground the crater deforms is an analytic map over a mesh (`sr_3d::crater`), not a material. Voxel ground is the first time the crater can be matter that is removed.
- sr-volume already has `SparseGrid` (f32 bricks, `Transform`, `CacheLimits`, the `.srvol` reader/writer). An occupancy grid can be a `SparseGrid` thresholded, or a bitset on the same brick layout; I would not make a second container.

## 1. Rigid body from the grid (V.4)

Definition: a body has `cells` (set of integer keys), `cellSize` s (metres, or scene units over `pixelsPerMeter`) and `density` ρ (kg/m3).

- **Mass, centre, inertia, exact.** Keep integer moments over the cells in a fixed order: N, Σi, Σj, Σk, Σi², Σj², Σk², Σij, Σik, Σjk (i128; a 1024^3 grid fits). Then m = N ρ s³; centre of mass = s (Σ(i,j,k)/N + ½); inertia tensor about the centre = ρ s⁵ [ N/6 I + second central moments of the cell centres ] (a cube about its own centre is m s²/6 on every axis; the cross terms come from the integer moments). All divisions come last, so the result is the same bits in any order of the cells and any number of threads. Principal axes by a symmetric Jacobi eigen-decomposition with a fixed sweep order (rapier wants principal inertia and a frame); degenerate (equal) eigenvalues give a fixed frame (identity), stated.
- **Use of rapier's own:** build the collider with `.density(ρ)` so rapier computes `mass_properties_voxels`, and CHECK it against the exact integer version in a test (1e-12). The integer version is also what the world needs for fragments (2) without building a collider first. Not `.mass(b.mass)`: a voxel body is given `density`, and `mass` together with `density` is an error (new rule).
- **Axes:** physics axes are a half turn about x of the scene's (x,y,z)->(x,-y,-z) (`flip`). For cells that is a lattice-exact map (i,j,k)->(i,-j-1,-k-1): no resampling, no off-by-half cell. Non-uniform scale of an object is a per-axis `voxel_size` (parry supports it); a rotation is the body's.
- **Collider options and cost:**
  (A) parry `Voxels` (recommended): exact union of cubes, concave, internal-face handling in the neighbourhood table, edit in place. NOT MEASURED: build time and step cost for 10^4, 10^5, 10^6 cells, a voxel body on a voxel floor, on the 51k-triangle crater trimesh; whether the manifold count per contact makes stacking jitter at 1/60 s steps. This is the first thing to measure (a probe like the ones that found the 41 ms trimesh build).
  (B) greedy-merged boxes in a compound: far fewer shapes for solid blocks, but ghost contacts at the seams of boxes that were one surface, and the compound's tree is rebuilt on an edit; use only if (A) is too slow for the big static terrain.
  (C) convex hull: wrong for anything concave (a bowl, a ring). Not offered.
- **Static/kinematic voxel ground** is a body with the same shape and no mass; its edits (crater removal) are applied to the collider with `set_voxel` between steps.

## 2. Destruction (V.5)

### 2.1 `crater@source` on a voxel object: the crater is removal
- A cell is removed when its centre is inside the bowl of the crater the law gives: depth below the pre-impact surface along the crater axis at most `depth * bowl(r/R)` (the same profile as `sr_3d::crater` with its exponent, so the voxel crater and the analytic crater are the same shape). Quantised: the removed volume is N s³ and differs from the law's V by at most the number of cells on the bowl surface times s³/2 (an oracle with that bound, below).
- **What the law's budget becomes in cells** (the work of 3.3, now exact in integers): remove the bowl (volume V); throw out the law's 0.8 V as particles; put the other 0.2 V back as cells on the rim (a deterministic ring, nearest empty cells above the surface in order of distance to the crest) so that cells removed = cells thrown + cells re-added to the cell. That makes the `bulking` factor a ratio of counts. The mantle and `crater@repose` bed are for the analytic ground; on voxel ground the debris that settles should become cells (a cellular relaxation at the angle of repose that moves cells: exact conservation by integers), which retires the height field for this case. Not for v1.
- **Ejecta from the removed cells instead of Housen's launch points?** Yes for mass and place, no for speed. Mass: each ejected cell, or a fixed group of m cells in scan order, is a particle of exactly its cells' mass, born where the cells were: the sum of the particles' masses is the removed mass to the last bit of the same expression (1e-12 oracle). Speed: the energy and momentum scaling is not derivable from the cells; keep the law's mass-speed distribution (cumulative mass above v falls as v^(-3 mu), already tested in `cumulative_mass_against_speed_has_the_slope_minus_three_mu`) by a quantile map: cells ordered by launch radius (nearest first) take the speeds in order, so the particle masses follow the law's distribution to a cell. Angles from the engine's model (`angle`, `angleSpread`). The result is deterministic (fixed order) and conserves mass exactly, which the sampled Housen list does not.
- **Fragments.** After the removal, connected components of what is left (6-connectivity) that do not contain an anchor (the static/kinematic cells, or for a dynamic body the largest component) separate. Each becomes its own body with mass N_i ρ s³, its own centre and inertia (integer moments, 1) and the velocity of the parent at its centre, v + ω × (c_i − c) (so linear momentum and angular momentum about the parent's centre are conserved at the split: oracle). Components under `fragmentMinCells` do not become bodies: they become particles (dust), mass kept.
- **Bodies that appear at run time vs the fixed world.** Two ways, the second recommended:
  (i) spawn in the world at the impact step: the body set is part of the checkpoint (handles are generational: replay from a checkpoint before the spawn must re-create the same handles in the same order, and the spawn must be a pure function of the state and the event). Doable but it changes `State`, the checkpoint charge, `Frame3` (body count varies by step) and everything that indexes bodies by position (evaluator, groups, links, ocean coupling, pyro). Large.
  (ii) **slots**: the scene says `maxFragments` N (default 64) and the world creates N disabled bodies at build, as it does for fracture pieces; a split at step n enables slot k with its shape, mass properties and velocity, in component order. `Collider::set_shape` exists (`shape_mut` is already used for the deforming crater collider). Bounded memory, no change to the body count, the same replay story as `fractured`. Components beyond N, or a second impact on a fragment, are an error that names `maxFragments` or, with `fragmentOverflow="dust"`, are dust. I would build (ii).
- **Determinism.** Labelling by scan order (x fastest, then y, then z) with union-find in that order; component k = the k-th by its smallest cell index; slots assigned in that order; masses are products of integers and one double; sums over cells are integer. Same bits for any thread count and any order of asking frames; the removal itself is a function of (impact point and axis, law, grid), computed from the impact event the world already logs.

### 2.2 `fracture@source` on a voxel object: the partition is a partition of cells
- Pieces = a partition of the cell set (Voronoi by fixed seeds over the cell centres, or planes, or authored labels); each part split into its connected components (a part may be disconnected). All of it is known at build: no spawn problem; the pieces are slots exactly as the existing mesh pieces are (`Geometry.pieces`, `enabled`, `fractured`). Piece mass/centre/inertia from integer moments; the sum of the pieces' masses equals the body's (exact in counts).
- `minImpulse` / `energyFraction` (FRX5 to FRX7) unchanged; the `source` for voxels is the object itself.

## 3. Sequences, revisions and cache (V.4/V.5 side of `srvseq`)

- Two kinds of time-varying occupancy: (a) **authored** frames (an animated voxel model: `srvseq`), (b) **simulated** edits (crater removals, splits, later deposits). Do not store a full occupancy per frame for either. State at time t = base grid ∘ edit log up to t, where an edit is (step, set of cell keys removed/added, in scan order) and the **revision** of a state is a hash of (base hash, the edits up to t). Authored frames are the same thing with the edits given in the file (delta frames), with a key frame (full bricks) every K frames, as the bricks of `.srvol` already are.
- **Seek and replay equal to the bit**: the occupancy is integer and the quantities derived from it (mass, centre, inertia, components) are functions of integers in a fixed order, so equal occupancy is equal bits. For simulated edits the log is inside the rigid world's state: the checkpoint at step n holds the occupancy (RLE bricks) and the slot table; a replay re-derives the edits from the same events and must reproduce the same log (compare to the bit, as the `Absorbed` log and `put_settled` already do; a replay that differs is an error).
- **Cache by revision** for the derived products: mesh (Mercurio's V.3 greedy/marching mesh), collider, mass properties, components, keyed by (revision, cellSize, density). A seek to an earlier revision hits the cache or rebuilds from a key frame; the byte budget is counted with the checkpoints' (`checkpoint_charge`), and thinned like them.
- Format: `srvseq` = header (cell size, transform, base hash), key frames as sr-volume bricks, delta frames as (step, removed bricks, added bricks), limits as `CacheLimits` (max cells, bricks, bytes, frames). Saturno owns the asset/schema (V.1): I need from it the occupancy as an asset kind, the revision hash, and an in-memory `Occupancy` type that both rigid and render read; Mercurio (V.3) needs only the revision and a `bricks_changed(from, to)` to remesh incrementally.

## 4. Oracles (each is a test that fails before the code)

1. **Rest.** A voxel block (a, b, c cells, ρ) dropped on a floor from the same pose as a box of the same half extents and mass: at rest the same height to 1e-6 m and the same orientation; run at 1/60 and 1/240 s. Also an L-shaped voxel body that must settle on the face the analytic centre of mass picks. (Risk: rapier's penetration allowance applies to both; if the two differ by more than 1e-6 the bound becomes the allowance, stated.)
2. **Mass properties.** Exact integer m, centre, inertia of a box (b²+c²)/12 m to 1e-12; of an L and of a hollow shell against hand-computed values; against parry's `mass_properties_voxels` to 1e-12; invariant to the order of the cells and to a shuffle.
3. **Components.** Two cubes sharing only an edge or a corner are 2 components (6-connectivity); a ring is 1, cut once is 1, cut twice is 2; a hollow shell with a sealed cavity is 1; a diagonal staircase of n cells is n; labels invariant under a shuffle of the input; component k is the k-th by smallest cell index.
4. **Removal.** |N_removed s³ − V| ≤ (cells on the bowl surface) s³ / 2 for the authored rock's law; the budget closes in counts (removed = thrown + re-added); the shape equals `sr_3d::crater`'s bowl to one cell.
5. **Fragments.** Σ m_i = m_removed + m_parent_left to 1e-12 of the mass; linear momentum and angular momentum about the parent's centre conserved at the split to 1e-12; ejecta mass = removed cells' mass to 1e-12; ejecta mass-speed slope within the law's.
6. **Determinism.** Same bits of every body frame for 1/2/8 threads, for forward play and for a scrub (forward, back, forward, checkpoint thinned), and for a fresh evaluator at the same time.
7. **Cost.** Step time against cell count (10^4, 10^5, 10^6) for a voxel body on a voxel floor and on the crater mesh; checkpoint size; the cost of a removal (edit + components + slot activation) for 10^3 and 10^5 removed cells.

## 5. Risks, cost, order of work

Risks: (1) the cost of the native voxel shape at 10^5+ cells and of cloning it into checkpoints is not measured, and decides between A and B; (2) stacking/jitter of voxel contacts (many manifold points) at the step sizes of the scenes; (3) slots bound the number of fragments: a crater in terrain makes many small islands, so `fragmentMinCells` and dust are not optional; (4) two ground models (analytic kernel and cells) coexist: `crater@mantle`/`bulking`/`repose` are for the analytic ground and a voxel owner has to refuse them (rule), until the settled debris becomes cells; (5) the render needs a remesh per revision (Mercurio) and an ocean over voxel terrain needs the bed from cells (the ocean's bed collider uses `crater::at`, not cells); (6) any dense grid is large: a 512^3 occupancy is 16 MiB as a bitset, so sparse bricks and budgets from the first commit; (7) the 180-degree axis flip and the half-cell offsets are classic off-by-half errors: oracle 1 on a block that is not symmetric.

Order (each step shippable, with its oracles): P1 `Occupancy` + integer moments + components + mass properties (pure, tests 2 and 3); P2 the rapier voxel collider behind `shape="voxels"` + the rest oracle + the cost probe (1 and 7); P3 slots and splitting at a given step (5, 6), without a crater; P4 crater removal and cell ejecta (4); P5 `fracture@source` partition; P6 `srvseq` sequences and revision cache. P1 and P2 do not depend on Saturno or Mercurio beyond the `Occupancy` type.

Names (to agree with Saturno and Mercurio): `rigidBody@shape="voxels"` (+ `rigidBody@density`, with `mass` an error: new rule), `rigidBody@maxFragments` (default 64), `rigidBody@fragmentMinCells`, `rigidBody@fragmentOverflow` (error | dust), `crater@source` on a voxel owner = removal (no new attribute; CRT5's "static/kinematic, auto or trimesh" gains `voxels`), `fracture@partition` (voronoi | planes | labels) for voxel sources, asset kind `voxelModel` / `voxelSequence` (V.1). Mine to define: the rules for the above, the `Occupancy`-to-rapier path, the splitting, the removal and the ejecta; theirs: the asset, `srvseq`, the mesher.
